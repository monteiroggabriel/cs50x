Using simple averages of energy, valence, and danceability across the top 100 songs may not be very representative for several reasons:

1. A listener might have a few extremely high-energy songs and many calm ones, but the average would show a moderate energy level, hiding that contrast.

2. The top 100 songs are treated equally, but the listener may have played some songs hundreds of times and others only a few times. A song played on
repeat likely reflects the listener's mood more than one played once.

3. A listener's taste may shift throughout the year (e.g., upbeat in summer, melancholic in winter). Averaging across the entire year erases those seasonal
or emotional phases.

4. Energy, valence, and danceability are just three of many audio features. Ignoring features like acousticness, instrumentalness, speechiness, or tempo means
the aura misses important dimensions of the listener's taste.

Better approaches could include:

- Weighted averages based on play count or listening time.

- Clustering songs into groups (e.g., using k-means) to identify distinct "moods" in the listener's library, rather than blending everything into one average.

- Time-weighted analysis that gives more importance to recently or frequently played songs.
